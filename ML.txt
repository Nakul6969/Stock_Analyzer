**"Why did you choose Random Forest?"**

Random Forest was the best fit for this project for 4 reasons:

1. **Small dataset** — We only had 30 stocks for training. Random Forest works well even with small data, unlike Deep Learning which needs thousands of samples.

2. **Mixed features** — Our 17 features are very different from each other (ratios, percentages, absolute values). Random Forest handles mixed feature types without needing normalization.

3. **No overfitting** — Since we have 30 samples and 17 features, overfitting is a real risk. Random Forest uses 200 trees and averaging to reduce overfitting automatically.

4. **Feature importance** — Random Forest tells us which features matter most (beta, volatility, PE ratio etc.), which is valuable for a finance project and makes a great slide in the presentation.

Viva Questions & Answers:Q: What is the target variable in your model?
A: `stdDev` from the API's riskMeter — standard deviation of stock returns, which measures how much a stock price fluctuates. We normalized it to a 1-10 scale using min-max normalization.

Q: What is min-max normalization?
A: It scales values to a fixed range. Formula: `(value - min) / (max - min) * 9 + 1`. So the most volatile stock gets score 10 and the least volatile gets score 1.

Q: What is Random Forest?
A: An ensemble of multiple decision trees. Each tree trains on a random subset of data and features. The final prediction is the average of all trees. More trees = more stable predictions.

Q: What is RMSE?
A: Root Mean Squared Error — measures average prediction error. If RMSE is 0.8, our risk score predictions are off by ±0.8 on average. Lower is better.

Q: What is R² score?
A: Measures how well the model explains the variance in data. R²=1.0 means perfect prediction, R²=0 means the model is no better than just predicting the average.

Q: Why not use Deep Learnin
A: Deep Learning needs thousands of samples to train well. We only have 30 stocks. Random Forest gives better results on small structured tabular data.

Q: Why not use Linear Regression?
A: Linear Regression assumes a straight-line relationship between features and risk. Stock risk is non-linear — a PE ratio of 50 doesn't mean exactly double the risk of PE 25. Random Forest captures these non-linear patterns.

Q: What is Beta in your features?
A: Beta measures how much a stock moves compared to the overall market. Beta > 1 means the stock is more volatile than the market. Beta < 1 means it's more stable. It's a key risk indicator.

Q: What is 50 DMA ratio?
A: Current price divided by the 50-day moving average. If ratio > 1, the stock is trading above its 50-day average (bullish signal). If ratio < 1, it's below the average (bearish, higher risk).

Q: How do you handle missing data?
A: We use default fallback values for missing features — for example, if PE ratio is missing, we use 20.0 as default (market average). The `fillna(median)` call also fills gaps during training.

Q: Can your model predict any stock?
A: Yes. The model learned patterns between financial features and risk — not the stock names. So it can predict risk for any stock as long as the API returns its financial data.

Q: What are the limitations of your model?
A: Three main ones — trained on only 30 stocks so accuracy improves with more data, market conditions change so the model needs periodic retraining, and stdDev is a proxy for risk not a perfect measure.

Q: What is the difference between your ML risk score and the API's risk label?
A: The API's `riskMeter` gives a category name like "Moderately High" based on their proprietary formula. Our ML model gives a precise numerical score from 1-10 based on 17 financial features that we selected and trained ourselves. Both are shown side by side in our UI for comparison.
